What the ATO asks for

  • A record of every hour you work from home, for the whole income year, kept at the time: a timesheet, roster, diary or a log of start and finish times like this one. Since 1 March 2023 the ATO does not accept an estimate or a four week sample.
  • The fixed rate is 70 cents an hour for 2024–25 and 2025–26 (67 cents for the two years before, 52 cents before that). It covers electricity and gas, internet, phone, stationery and computer consumables. Things like a desk or laptop are claimed separately with their own records.
  • Cents are disregarded, not rounded. 136 hours at 70 cents is $95.20, which is claimed as $95.
  • 2026–27 is provisional. The ATO had not published a rate for the current year when this page was written, so it uses 70 cents and says so until the rate is confirmed.
  • Entries made after the day are marked. Only a record kept at the time counts, so anything added later shows "added later" on the screen and in the report.

Read the rules on the ATO's fixed rate method page. You also need one record of each running expense you pay for, such as a bill. This page helps you keep the records; your tax agent or the ATO decides what you can claim.

Good to know

  • The ATO has a free app too. myDeductions, inside the ATO app, can record hours worked from home. This page is for people who would rather use a laptop, skip the myGov sign in, keep timesheets for clients as well, or want files they keep.
  • Everything stays on this device. Nothing is uploaded and there is no account, so use Backup file to keep a copy or move to another device.
  • Works offline. After your first visit the page opens with no connection, and the timer keeps its start time if you close the tab.
