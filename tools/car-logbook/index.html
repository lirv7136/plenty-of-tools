      What the ATO asks for

      • At least 12 continuous weeks, representative of how you use the car across the year.
      • Odometer readings at the start and end of the logbook period, and the total kilometres in between.
      • For each work journey: the purpose, the destination, the odometer at the start and end, and the kilometres. Two or more journeys in a row on the same day can be one entry, which is what "Combine same day journeys" does.
      • Entries at the time, at the end of the journey or as soon as possible afterwards. This page records when each trip was entered and marks anything added more than a week later, so there are no surprises at tax time.
      • Business use percentage is work kilometres divided by total kilometres for the period. A logbook stays valid for five years; in the later years keep the odometer readings at the start and end of each income year.

      Read the rules on the ATO's logbook method page. This page helps you keep the records; your tax agent or the ATO decides what you can claim.

      Good to know

      • The ATO has a free app too. myDeductions, inside the ATO app, logs car trips on your phone. This page is for people who would rather use a laptop as well as a phone, skip the myGov sign in, or want a printed report and files they keep.
      • No automatic GPS tracking. A web page stops when your phone locks, so it cannot log trips in the background. You type the odometer readings, which is what the ATO asks for anyway. Apps that detect trips automatically are what the paid logbook apps sell.
      • Everything stays on this device. Trips live in this browser's storage. Nothing is uploaded and there is no account, so use Backup file to keep a copy or move to another device.
      • Works offline. After your first visit the page opens with no connection. Add it to your home screen and it behaves like an app.
