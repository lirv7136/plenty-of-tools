# Product Hunt launch draft

Launch on a Tuesday, Wednesday or Thursday at 12:01 am Pacific, which is 5:01 pm Sydney on the
same day. Schedule it in the break (28 September to 9 October), not the same day as Show HN.
Have five friends ready to leave real comments in the first hour; do not ask for upvotes.

**Name:** Plenty of Tools

**Tagline (60 characters max):**

```
Free browser tools for things you usually pay for. No uploads.
```

**Topics:** Productivity, Privacy, Open Source, Design Tools

**Description:**

> Plenty of Tools is a set of small, single purpose tools that each replace a paid product
> at the exact step where it charges you: the CV builder that charges to download, the
> recorder with a time cap, the PDF signer that watermarks, the photo converter that uploads
> your pictures to its servers.
>
> Every tool runs entirely in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, there are no accounts and no
> ads, and the code is MIT licensed on GitHub. Each tool comes with an honest "is it worth
> paying for X?" page, including the cases where the answer is yes.
>
> Live today: CV Builder, Subscription Finder, Screen Recorder, Background Remover, Sign a PDF,
> QR Codes, Australian Invoice Generator, GPX Route Builder, Image Converter (HEIC to JPG with
> metadata removal), Is It Worth It calculators, Browser Transcription, Settle Up (expense
> splitting with the ledger in the share link) and a Tuner and Metronome.

**Maker's first comment:**

> Hi Product Hunt. I'm Lachlan, a final year engineering student in Sydney. I started this
> after noticing how many everyday tools now put their one useful action behind a subscription:
> download the CV, record past five minutes, sign the PDF, convert the photo.
>
> The bet is that a browser can do all of these for free because there is no server to pay
> for. So the rule for every tool here is: runs in your browser, nothing uploaded, no account,
> open source. I'd rather you paid for the products that earn it (the comparison pages say
> when that is) and kept your money for the rest.
>
> Things I'd love feedback on: which tool you'd actually use, what's missing, and anything that
> breaks. Everything is on GitHub if you want to poke at it.
